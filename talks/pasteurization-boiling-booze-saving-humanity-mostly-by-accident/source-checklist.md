# Source Checklist

This deck is a lively talk artifact, not a finished academic reference. Before it gets reused as formal science history or public health teaching material, give it a source pass.

That does not mean the talk is wrong. It means the talk was built to work in a room, and documents that leave the room need sturdier shoes.

## Claims To Verify Before Republishing

- Napoleon III's role in commissioning Pasteur to study wine spoilage.
- The French wine export context and any connection to trade with Britain.
- The fermentation timeline: Lille, M. Bigot, lactic acid fermentation, alcoholic fermentation, and the Pasteur effect.
- The dates and temperature ranges for wine, beer, and milk pasteurization.
- Any numeric impact claims, especially public health or trade numbers.
- The chicken cholera story, including the difference between the polished vacation story and Pasteur's notebook record.
- The Pouilly-le-Fort anthrax vaccine trial, Toussaint's role, and the chemical inactivation issue.
- The rabies vaccine treatment of Joseph Meister and the ethical risk context.
- Pasteur's rivalries, reputation management, and authoritarian behavior claims.

## Presenter Voice To Label Clearly

These lines are useful on stage, but shouldn't be mistaken for historical quotations:

- "Hold my petri dish."
- "Science by procrastination."
- "Made brunch mathematically safer."
- "French chemist with a microscope and an attitude problem."
- "The porcelain gods."
- "Before him, every sip was more of a dare than modern drinkers like to admit." Stage hyperbole about the danger of drinking wine, not a historical claim.

The jokes help people remember the point. Just make sure the joke and the citation aren't wearing the same hat.

## Useful Final Slide Structure

A cleaned-up version of the talk should probably end with a source slide organized this way:

- Primary Pasteur works and translations
- Modern biographies and history of science sources
- Public health and food safety sources
- Wine, beer, and milk pasteurization references
- Sources on Toussaint, Roux, Chamberland, Koch, and the vaccine disputes

## Cleanup Note

The current exported deck text includes slide text, presenter notes, research notes, and a few placeholder claims in one stream. Separate those before turning the talk into a public article or handout.

Nobody needs to know how the sausage got made unless the sausage is the lesson.
